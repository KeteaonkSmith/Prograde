Why opportunities matter

The goal is not just to look impressive. It is to learn, contribute, and show real evidence.

Explore

Test a field before committing to it.

A summer course or competition can show whether you really enjoy AI, policy, math, design, research, or entrepreneurship before you commit harder.

Build

Create proof of skill.

Projects, papers, presentations, code, awards, and mentor feedback carry more weight than saying you are interested in something.

Access

Find paths your school may not advertise.

Free programs, scholarships, and open resources can open doors that expensive private prep often tries to gatekeep.

Why students use it

Built for the moment when you know you should be doing something, but do not know where to start.

01

Plain-English search

Describe your grade, interests, budget, location, and constraints in normal language. The site is built around how students actually ask for help.

02

Decision-ready cards

Every card shows cost, deadline, eligibility, effort, and why it may fit. You should not have to open five tabs just to learn something is too expensive or already closed.

03

Human verification

Listings are checked for stale deadlines, hidden fees, and eligibility traps. Prograde is meant to save students from wasting time on bad leads.

How to use Prograde

Turn vague ambition into a short list you can actually use.

Step 1

Set your profile

Pick your grade, interests, cost tolerance, preferred format, and effort level. The catalog starts to feel personal instead of generic.

Step 2

Compare the tradeoffs

Look at cost, deadline, effort, format, and eligibility before opening tabs. Skip anything that is prestigious but wrong for you.

Step 3

Verify and apply

Every resource card points to its official source. Use Prograde to narrow the field, then confirm dates and requirements before you submit anything.

Best time to apply

Most "summer" opportunities are won months before summer.

Check current status
9th-10th

Build foundations

Use free courses, competitions, clubs, and small projects. Do not wait for a prestigious program to give you permission to start.

Junior fall

Shortlist serious programs

Research-heavy programs and selective summer opportunities often need essays, recommendations, and problem sets before winter.

Junior winter

Submit summer applications

Many competitive summer programs close between December and March, even if the program happens in June or July.

Senior fall

Scholarship season

Major national scholarships often open in August and close by September-November. Summer before senior year is prep time.
