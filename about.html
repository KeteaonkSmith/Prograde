Why this exists

Prograde is for students who know they should be doing something, but hate starting in a maze.

Too many good opportunities are scattered across stale pages, buried PDFs, generic scholarship lists, and official sites that assume you already know what to look for. Prograde is meant to reduce that gap between ambition and access.

What Prograde actually does

It gives students a cleaner way to find, judge, and keep using academic opportunities.

Guide first

Start with a real ranked page.

The strongest pages on Prograde are not wrappers around search. They are actual guides with notes, tradeoffs, costs, deadlines, and honest recommendations.

Catalog second

Use the broader field when you need it.

The catalog helps once you want to compare more options, filter by fit, and keep official sources close.

Dashboard third

Turn the shortlist into momentum.

The dashboard is there so a useful search does not disappear the next morning. It keeps saved opportunities, study sessions, and next steps visible.

Why I built it

Students are constantly told to show initiative, then left alone with bad search infrastructure.

A lot of the advice students hear sounds simple until they try to act on it. “Find programs.” “Apply to scholarships.” “Build a strong profile.” Then the real experience is twenty tabs, mixed deadlines, hidden costs, eligibility traps, and no clue what is actually worth the time.

Prograde is supposed to lower the cost of choosing. It should help a student get to something real faster, understand why it matters, and move before the window closes.

01

Specific beats polished.

Students need deadlines, cost notes, fit caveats, and plain-English warnings more than they need another glossy landing page.

02

Prestige is not the only filter.

The point is not to chase the flashiest name. The point is to find something worth doing well.

03

Small next steps matter.

Especially for neurodivergent students or students under stress, one visible next action beats ten abstract goals.

04

Students should be able to help each other.

The best version of Prograde keeps notes, shortcuts, and useful warnings flowing forward instead of making every student start from zero.

How to read the product

This is not a grading tool, and it is not pretending to be one.

Student-facing

Built for students, not teacher workflows.

Prograde is about scholarships, summer programs, competitions, campus visits, courses, and tools that move students forward.

Honest

Fit sorts. It does not predict.

A ranking can help you narrow the field, but it cannot guarantee admission, scholarship success, or selection.

Verified outward

Official pages still control the truth.

Prograde should help you think better and move faster, but the final source of deadlines and eligibility is still the organization running the opportunity.

Community goal

The site should help students do things that matter, then help the next student start faster.

Access

Make hidden opportunities easier to find.

Saving another student time, confusion, and missed deadlines is already real community value.

Translation

Explain what a page means in plain English.

A lot of students do not need more links. They need someone to say what an opportunity is, who it is for, and who should skip it.

Give-back

Turn what you learn into help for someone else.

The longer-term goal is a site where student notes, local guides, and practical advice make opportunity less dependent on insider help.
